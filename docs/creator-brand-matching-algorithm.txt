MADLIPZ V2: CREATOR-BRAND MATCHING ALGORITHM — REVISED
Design Report — Revision 2, 2026-06-08
================================================================================

REVISION LOG
============

This revision addresses five flaws identified in external review of the original
winning design (Revision 1, 2026-06-08). The tournament result stands — Approach
1 (Multilingual Embedding Similarity) remains the winner. All changes are
contained within the winning design's implementation specification; the
elimination decisions, hard constraints, and tournament judgment are unaffected.

  FIX 1 — Score formula restructured. Hard fidelity floor added; multiplicative
           modifier chain replaced with a bounded additive modifier. Cosine
           similarity dominates ranking by construction. Worked example
           recomputed.

  FIX 2 — Maturity counted once. profile_weight removed from the score formula.
           creator_profile_floor hard WHERE gate is the sole maturity mechanism.
           Within the admitted cohort, ranking is by fidelity and performance
           only.

  FIX 3 — Shared embedding space redefined. The "shared linear projection layer"
           is eliminated. Both creator vectors and brief vectors are encoded by
           the same multilingual sentence transformer with no learned alignment
           step. Cold-start comparability is a native property of the model's
           semantic space, not a trained artifact.

  FIX 4 — Diversity guard rewritten. Categorical language_cluster field
           eliminated; that field was undefined and contradicted the
           continuous-geometry premise of Section 1. Replaced with Maximal
           Marginal Relevance (MMR) over embedding distance — a geometry-native
           diversity pass requiring no discrete language label.

  FIX 5 — Upgrade path promoted to a first-class section. The "Earned Re-Rank
           Layer" (Section 6) defines the correct architectural verdict
           ("embedding now, learned re-rank later"), concrete trigger conditions,
           and an attachment mechanism that does not fork the embedding engine.

WINNING DESIGN: MULTILINGUAL EMBEDDING SIMILARITY (REVISED)
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------

The engine operates on a single 768-dimensional embedding space where creator
voice profiles and brand brief requirements are represented as dense vectors
produced by the same multilingual sentence transformer. Cosine similarity
between these vectors is the primary scoring primitive and the dominant ranking
signal. Both Voice Syndicate surfaces — creator-facing (here is a sports moment
to react to) and brand-facing (here is a creator whose audience matches your
brief) — query the same materialized score table with different filter
predicates. There is no fork.

Revision 2 change summary: (a) the score formula now enforces a hard fidelity
floor and uses a bounded additive modifier so cosine similarity cannot be
overridden by secondary signals; (b) maturity is enforced exactly once, at the
WHERE clause, not in the score; (c) both sides of the embedding comparison use
the same transformer with no learned alignment layer; (d) the diversity guard
uses geometric MMR instead of categorical language clusters; and (e) the upgrade
path to a learned re-ranker is specified as a first-class architectural phase.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: INPUTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CREATOR SIDE
------------

A creator embedding is a 768-dimensional dense vector computed as the
salience-weighted mean of multilingual sentence transformer encodings
(paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2, or a fine-tuned sport-domain variant)
over all of a creator's remix clips. Each clip's embedding is derived from its
transcript text and label tokens concatenated into a single input string. The
transformer encodes this string directly — no downstream projection.

The resulting vector occupies a continuous, culturally-graded position in the
shared embedding space. A creator producing 70% Spanish and 30% Portuguese
content occupies a position in embedding space that reflects both, preserving
the cultural-linguistic texture. Language is NOT a categorical flag — it is a
geometric location in a space trained on hundreds of millions of multilingual
examples.

Schema columns that feed the creator embedding:

  - remix_labels.label_id joined to labels.key / labels.displayName
    (confidence-weighted; source='human' labels get 1.5x weight over 'llm')
  - remix_labels.confidence (scales each label's contribution)
  - remixes.mode (reaction vs. dub signals cultural commentary vs. vocal
    performance; feeds style dimension of embedding)
  - clips.salienceScore (mean and variance; weights clip contributions to
    the embedding mean)
  - clips.visibility (only 'public' records feed the embedding)

Additional scalar signals computed per creator and stored alongside the
embedding (not embedded; applied at ranking time):

  dark_social_ratio:
    private-channel click events / total share events, 30-day rolling window.
    Private-channel: referrer IS NULL or matches WhatsApp/Telegram/iMessage
    UA patterns on madlipz.to/{short-id} watermark link clicks.
    Range: [0.0, 1.0]. Stored in creator_embeddings table.

  normalized_salience:
    Creator's mean clips.salienceScore, normalized to [0,1] across active
    creator population. Stored in creator_embeddings table.

  profile_weight: [RETAINED FOR ANALYTICS ONLY — NOT USED IN SCORE FORMULA]
    Stored in creator_embeddings for reporting. Removed from score computation
    per FIX 2. Maturity is enforced by the creator_profile_floor hard gate;
    this column is not consulted at ranking time.

BRAND SIDE
----------

Brand brief embedding: a 768-dimensional dense vector produced by the SAME
multilingual sentence transformer used for creator embeddings, with NO learned
projection layer. [FIX 3]

The brief's structured fields are composed into a natural-language prompt string
and encoded directly:

  Input text assembled from required fields at brief creation time:
    - target_language_codes text[]
      Language names are converted to human-readable strings
      (e.g., ["es", "pt"] → "Spanish Portuguese") and included in the input.
    - target_sentiment text
      Free-text description of desired emotional register (e.g., "high-energy
      celebration," "respectful commentary"). Included verbatim.
    - content_category_ids uuid[]
      FK references resolved to labels.displayName values and included as
      comma-separated tokens.
    - campaign_sport text, campaign_moment_type text
      Included as content tokens.

  Example assembled string for a brief targeting Spanish football celebration:
    "Spanish language sports football championship celebration high-energy
    vocal commentary audience excitement"

  This string is encoded with paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2 to produce
  the brief's 768-dimensional embedding.

WHY CREATOR AND BRIEF VECTORS ARE COMPARABLE WITHOUT A PROJECTION LAYER:

  Multilingual sentence transformers are trained with contrastive objectives
  over naturally occurring text pairs at massive scale. The model learns to
  place semantically similar content close in its output space regardless of
  surface form. A creator whose remixes consist of "¡Increíble gol! ¡Campeones!"
  with label tokens "football, Spanish, celebration" and a brief assembled as
  "Spanish football championship high-energy celebration" produce similar vectors
  because the model has encountered millions of semantically equivalent pairs
  across dozens of languages during pretraining. No alignment layer is needed or
  appropriate: the transformer IS the alignment. Adding a learned linear
  projection between the two sides would require paired (creator, brief)
  conversion outcome data that does not exist at launch, creating a cold-start
  failure for the core similarity primitive. Eliminating the projection means
  the comparison is valid on day zero.

  creator_profile_floor text (enum) — BRAND BRIEF FIELD, NOT CREATOR FIELD
    Values: aspirational_ok | blended_min | data_derived_only
    Applied as a hard WHERE gate at retrieval time. This is the sole place where
    profile maturity enters the system. It is not a score modifier.

Brief embeddings are computed at creation time and recomputed on any edit.

POSTGRES STORAGE
----------------

  creator_embeddings (
    user_id              uuid PRIMARY KEY,
    embedding            vector(768),        -- pgvector column; blended result
    prior_embedding      vector(768),        -- survey-derived prior; set at registration, never overwritten
    remix_count          int     DEFAULT 0,  -- drives blend ratio in creator_embedding_refresh
    cold_start           bool    DEFAULT true,
    dark_social_ratio    float,
    normalized_salience  float,
    profile_weight       float,              -- analytics only, not used in score
    updated_at           timestamptz
  )

  brand_brief_embeddings (
    brief_id             uuid PRIMARY KEY,
    embedding            vector(768),
    fidelity_threshold   float   DEFAULT 0.45,  -- per-brief floor; read by compute_match_scores
    updated_at           timestamptz
  )

  match_scores (
    creator_id       uuid,
    brief_id         uuid,
    cosine_sim       float,                  -- stored separately for floor check
    final_score      float,
    computed_at      timestamptz,
    PRIMARY KEY (creator_id, brief_id)
  )

  -- Phase 2 table (defined now, populated when Section 6 triggers are met):
  rerank_scores (
    creator_id       uuid,
    brief_id         uuid,
    rerank_score     float,
    model_version    text,
    computed_at      timestamptz,
    PRIMARY KEY (creator_id, brief_id)
  )

Indexes:
  IVFFlat on creator_embeddings.embedding
  IVFFlat on brand_brief_embeddings.embedding
  (brief_id, final_score DESC) on match_scores
  (creator_id, final_score DESC) on match_scores

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: SCORING AND RANKING MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SCORE FORMULA [FIX 1 + FIX 2]
------------------------------

The original formula was a pure multiplicative chain:

  ORIGINAL (superseded):
    final_score = cosine_sim × profile_weight × (1+0.3×dark_social)
                  × (0.5+0.5×salience)

  This formula had two structural defects:
  (a) The combined non-fidelity multiplier had a 6.5× dynamic range
      (0.4×1.0×0.5=0.20 to 1.0×1.3×1.0=1.30), while cosine_sim spans [0,1].
      A 0.5-fidelity mature/viral creator could outrank a 0.8-fidelity new
      creator by up to 4:1 — the exact violation of Constraint 2.
  (b) profile_weight double-penalized maturity that creator_profile_floor
      already gates as a hard WHERE clause.

The revised formula enforces fidelity dominance by two mechanisms:

  STEP A — Hard Fidelity Floor (pre-score gate):
    A creator is admitted to scoring only when:
      cosine_sim(creator_embedding, brief_embedding) >= brief.fidelity_threshold

    brief.fidelity_threshold is stored in brand_brief_embeddings.fidelity_threshold
    (DEFAULT 0.45) and is set by the brand at brief creation time via the brief
    submission form. Allowed range: [0.35, 0.55]. The compute_match_scores job
    reads this value from brand_brief_embeddings when it loads the brief record;
    no separate configuration lookup is needed.

    Permitted values by brief tier:
      - Broad discovery briefs: 0.35 (wider funnel, lower certainty of fit)
      - Standard briefs:        0.45 (default)
      - Precision audience:     0.55 (narrow funnel, high certainty required)

    Applied before the score formula — a creator below the floor does not receive
    a score and cannot appear in any shortlist regardless of how strong their
    dark-social or salience signals are.

    In a well-calibrated 768-dim sentence transformer space, cosine_sim < 0.45
    represents near-random semantic association. No amount of community reach
    or platform tenure justifies surfacing a creator whose content is
    semantically disconnected from the brief.

  STEP B — Bounded Additive Modifier (score formula for admitted creators):

    final_score = cosine_sim(creator_embedding, brief_embedding)
                × (1 + 0.15 × dark_social_ratio
                     + 0.10 × normalized_salience)

    Modifier properties:
      - Range of the parenthesized term: [1.00, 1.25]
        (minimum: dark_social=0, salience=0 → 1.00)
        (maximum: dark_social=1, salience=1 → 1+0.15+0.10=1.25)
      - Maximum possible boost from secondary signals: +25% of cosine_sim
      - To close the gap between a 0.8-fidelity creator and a 0.5-fidelity
        creator using secondary signals alone requires a 60% boost, which
        the formula's cap of 25% cannot produce. Fidelity dominates by
        construction.

    Interpretation of terms:
      - dark_social_ratio boost (max +15%): rewards creators whose content
        travels through private channels — authentic community resonance
        not attributable to algorithmic amplification.
      - normalized_salience boost (max +10%): rewards creators with
        consistently high-salience clips, reflecting content that holds
        audience attention.
      - profile_weight: REMOVED. Maturity is enforced exclusively by the
        creator_profile_floor WHERE gate (FIX 2). Within the admitted
        cohort, a new creator who passes the fidelity floor and a veteran
        who passes it are ranked purely on fidelity and performance signals.

WORKED EXAMPLE: OLD vs. NEW FORMULA [FIX 1]
--------------------------------------------

Scenario: A brief targets Spanish-language football celebration content with
high vocal energy.

  Creator A — New Spanish football creator
    cosine_sim = 0.82  (strong semantic match: Spanish, football, high-energy)
    profile_weight = 0.4 (aspirational; few remixes)
    dark_social_ratio = 0.30 (moderate private-channel sharing)
    normalized_salience = 0.40 (limited clip history)

  Creator B — Veteran English-language sports creator
    cosine_sim = 0.52  (partial match: sports adjacent, but English, not Spanish)
    profile_weight = 1.0 (data_derived; extensive history)
    dark_social_ratio = 0.75 (strong dark-social track record)
    normalized_salience = 0.90 (high-salience established catalog)

  ORIGINAL FORMULA (superseded):

    Creator A: 0.82 × 0.4 × (1+0.3×0.30) × (0.5+0.5×0.40)
             = 0.82 × 0.4 × 1.09 × 0.70
             = 0.250

    Creator B: 0.52 × 1.0 × (1+0.3×0.75) × (0.5+0.5×0.90)
             = 0.52 × 1.0 × 1.225 × 0.95
             = 0.605

    Creator B outranks A: 0.605 / 0.250 = 2.42:1
    CONSTRAINT 2 VIOLATION: The English creator beats the Spanish creator
    for a Spanish brief by more than 2:1. Language fidelity is not first-class.

  REVISED FORMULA:

    Fidelity floor check (FIDELITY_THRESHOLD = 0.45):
      Creator A: 0.82 >= 0.45 — ADMITTED
      Creator B: 0.52 >= 0.45 — ADMITTED

    Creator A: 0.82 × (1 + 0.15×0.30 + 0.10×0.40)
             = 0.82 × (1 + 0.045 + 0.040)
             = 0.82 × 1.085
             = 0.890

    Creator B: 0.52 × (1 + 0.15×0.75 + 0.10×0.90)
             = 0.52 × (1 + 0.1125 + 0.090)
             = 0.52 × 1.2025
             = 0.625

    Creator A outranks B: 0.890 > 0.625
    The Spanish-language new creator correctly ranks first. Even with Creator
    B at maximum dark-social (1.0) and maximum salience (1.0), their best
    possible score is 0.52 × 1.25 = 0.650, still below Creator A's baseline
    score of 0.82 × 1.00 = 0.820.

  EDGE CASE — Fidelity floor protecting against modifier-inflated low-fidelity:

    Creator C — viral creator, poor semantic match
      cosine_sim = 0.40  (marginal; different sport, different linguistic region)
      dark_social_ratio = 1.0  (extremely high dark-social)
      normalized_salience = 1.0  (very high salience)

    Fidelity floor check: 0.40 < 0.45 — EXCLUDED regardless of modifiers.

    Under original formula (no floor): 0.40 × 1.0 × 1.30 × 1.0 = 0.520
    Creator C would have ranked above Creator B in the old formula despite
    being semantically misaligned with the brief. The fidelity floor prevents
    this class of failure entirely.

RANKING PIPELINE
----------------

Step 1 — ANN Shortlist:
  Retrieve top-200 creators by cosine similarity using pgvector's <=> operator
  with the IVFFlat index. This step is approximate and fast. The per-brief
  fidelity_threshold is applied here as a WHERE clause:
    WHERE creator_profile_floor_condition(brief.creator_profile_floor,
                                          ce.profileState)
      AND 1 - (ce.embedding <=> be.embedding) >= brief.fidelity_threshold

Step 2 — Exact Re-Rank:
  Compute final_score for all admitted candidates using the revised formula:
    final_score = cosine_sim × (1 + 0.15 × dark_social_ratio
                                  + 0.10 × normalized_salience)
  Store cosine_sim and final_score separately in a working set (not yet
  persisted to match_scores at this step — persistence happens in the job).

Step 3 — Diversity Guard via MMR [FIX 4]:
  Apply Maximal Marginal Relevance selection over the re-ranked candidate set.
  This step is geometry-native: it uses the embedding vectors directly and
  requires no categorical language cluster field.

  MMR selection algorithm (application layer, applied to top-50 candidates
  from Step 2 to produce the final top-N):

    λ = 0.7  (configurable; 0.7 = 70% relevance weight, 30% diversity weight)
    N = 20   (default for brand-facing discovery)

    selected = []
    pool = candidates sorted by final_score DESC (up to 50)

    while len(selected) < N and pool is not empty:
      if selected is empty:
        best = pool[0]              // first pick is simply highest score
      else:
        best = argmax over pool:
          λ × normalize(c.final_score)
          − (1−λ) × max(cosine_sim(c.embedding, s.embedding)
                        for s in selected)
        // normalize: final_score / max(final_score in pool)

      append best to selected
      remove best from pool

    return selected

  Properties of this approach:
  - No "language_cluster" field is defined or consulted (eliminating the
    undefined categorical field that contradicted the continuous-geometry
    premise of Section 1).
  - Linguistic diversity emerges automatically: if the top-5 candidates all
    have near-identical embeddings (implying the same language/cultural region),
    their mutual cosine_sim scores are high, and the (1−λ) penalty term will
    favor the next candidate who occupies a different region of the space.
  - For narrowly-specified briefs (e.g., brief explicitly targets a single
    language), candidates will naturally have dispersed embeddings once that
    language cluster is exhausted, and MMR behaves like a relevance-only
    selection. The diversity mechanism activates exactly when it is needed:
    underspecified briefs with clustering risk.
  - λ is configurable per brief tier. Brands running explicit multi-language
    discovery briefs can reduce λ to 0.5 to maximize linguistic diversity.

Step 4 — Return top-N:
  Default N = 20 for brand-facing discovery; configurable per brief tier.
  If rerank_scores has entries for this brief (Section 6 Phase 2), the API
  layer substitutes rerank_score for the final ordering within the MMR set.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: COLD-START HANDLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NEW CREATORS (profileState = 'aspirational', zero remix history)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Zero remixes means no remix_labels, no salience scores, no dark-social events.
The creator embedding cannot be computed from content alone.

Strategy: prior embedding from onboarding survey + deterministic blending.

At registration:
  Creators complete a language and interest survey (max 5 taps). Survey
  responses are mapped to entries in the council-governed labels table. Those
  label display names are assembled into a text string and encoded with the same
  multilingual transformer used for all other embeddings. The resulting vector
  is written to TWO columns simultaneously:
    creator_embeddings.prior_embedding  ← set once at registration; never overwritten
    creator_embeddings.embedding        ← initially identical to prior_embedding; updated on each remix milestone

  cold_start is set to true and remix_count to 0 at registration.

  Because the prior is encoded by the same transformer as content embeddings
  and brief embeddings (FIX 3), the prior vector is immediately comparable to
  brief vectors via cosine_sim. A new creator who marks "Spanish, football,
  humor" in their survey lands in the same region of the 768-dimensional space
  as briefs targeting Spanish football entertainment — no training required.

  Scalar signals at cold start:
    dark_social_ratio = 0.0  (no share events yet)
    normalized_salience = 0.0  (no clips yet)
    Score modifier = 1 + 0.15×0 + 0.10×0 = 1.00

  A cold-start creator's final_score equals their cosine_sim exactly (modifier
  is 1.0). They are not zero-scored and not arbitrarily penalized beyond the
  maturity gate — they simply receive no secondary signal boost yet.

Blending schedule (shifts as remix_count grows):
  remix_count 0–4:   embedding = normalize(0.70 × prior_embedding + 0.30 × content_embedding)
  remix_count 5–9:   embedding = normalize(0.50 × prior_embedding + 0.50 × content_embedding)
  remix_count 10+:   embedding = normalize(0.20 × prior_embedding + 0.80 × content_embedding)
  (At remix_count = 10, profileState transitions toward 'blended'; cold_start set to false)

  content_embedding is computed fresh on each call as the salience-weighted mean
  of all clip encodings to date. prior_embedding is read from the stored column
  and never recomputed. The result is written to creator_embeddings.embedding.

A pg-boss job creator_embedding_refresh runs on remix.created events. It
increments remix_count, reads the updated value, selects the blend ratio from
the schedule above, computes the blended embedding, and upserts
creator_embeddings.embedding and creator_embeddings.remix_count. The
prior_embedding column is read but never written by this job.

Aspirational creators are only surfaced to briefs where:
  creator_profile_floor = 'aspirational_ok'
Brands opting into this signal seek discovery, not proven reach. This gate
protects brand trust while giving new creators market exposure. Within the
aspirational cohort, new creators compete on fidelity and organic performance,
not on tenure — the score formula contains no maturity penalty (FIX 2).

NEW BRANDS (zero prior campaigns)
----------------------------------

Brief embedding is always computed from required structured fields (language
targets, sentiment text, content categories are required at submission) —
a valid embedding always exists at brief creation time.

The dark-social multiplier term is zeroed for the first campaign:
  dark_social_ratio treated as 0.0 for all score computations.
This prevents over-indexing on creators whose dark-social signal is inflated
by unrelated viral events unconnected to the brand's category.

After the first campaign completes (click/share events accumulate), the full
formula applies using actual campaign-derived dark-social data.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: VOICE SYNDICATE UNIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The matching engine IS the Voice Syndicate engine. There is no separate
implementation. The data structures and computation paths are shared.

SHARED DATA STRUCTURE
---------------------

  match_scores (creator_id, brief_id, cosine_sim, final_score, computed_at)

This single table is the substrate for both surfaces. Brand-facing and
creator-facing surfaces are two query orientations over identical rows
computed by an identical formula. cosine_sim is stored separately from
final_score to enable fast floor checks and to support Phase 2 re-ranking
(Section 6) without recomputing embeddings.

SHARED PG-BOSS JOB
------------------

Job name: compute_match_scores
Payload: { scope: 'brief' | 'creator', id: uuid }

  scope = 'brief':
    1. Load brief embedding from brand_brief_embeddings.
    2. Execute ANN query with fidelity floor WHERE clause.
    3. Compute cosine_sim (exact) and final_score for all admitted creators.
    4. Upsert into match_scores via INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE,
       writing both cosine_sim and final_score.

  scope = 'creator':
    1. Load creator embedding from creator_embeddings.
    2. For all active briefs, compute cosine_sim and apply floor check.
    3. Compute final_score for admitted (brief, creator) pairs.
    4. Upsert into match_scores.

Both paths write to the same table. The job does not know or care which
surface will read the result.

JOB TRIGGERS
------------

  1. New brand brief published:
     compute_match_scores(scope: 'brief', id: brief_id) enqueued immediately.

  2. Creator remix milestone (5th, 10th, 25th remix):
     creator_embedding_refresh fires first, then
     compute_match_scores(scope: 'creator', id: creator_id) enqueued.

  3. Nightly stale-score sweep:
     All active briefs with computed_at < now() - interval '24 hours' are
     recomputed via compute_match_scores(scope: 'brief').

  4. Nightly rerank-score sweep (Phase 2 only; no-op before Section 6 triggers):
     All active briefs that have any rerank_scores rows are re-inferred using
     the current model version via compute_rerank_scores(scope: 'brief').
     This keeps rerank_scores current on the daily cadence that the Phase 2
     API query's freshness window requires. Job is registered at deploy time
     but produces zero work until the re-rank layer is activated.

BRAND-FACING SURFACE (creator discovery)
-----------------------------------------

  -- Phase 1 (embedding only):
  SELECT ms.creator_id, ms.final_score
  FROM match_scores ms
  WHERE ms.brief_id = $1
  ORDER BY ms.final_score DESC
  LIMIT $n;

  -- Phase 2 (after re-rank layer is active; Section 6):
  SELECT ms.creator_id,
         COALESCE(rs.rerank_score, ms.final_score) AS effective_score
  FROM match_scores ms
  LEFT JOIN rerank_scores rs
    ON rs.creator_id = ms.creator_id
   AND rs.brief_id = ms.brief_id
   AND rs.computed_at > now() - interval '36 hours'
  WHERE ms.brief_id = $1
  ORDER BY effective_score DESC
  LIMIT $n;

  The 36-hour freshness window is intentionally tighter than the nightly sweep
  cadence (24 hours) to allow for job delays without gaps. If compute_rerank_scores
  fails for a brief, the COALESCE falls back to final_score (embedding-only ranking)
  automatically — no silent degradation, no dead scores. An alert fires when
  rerank_scores rows for an active brief are absent or stale (monitored by the
  nightly sweep job's completion log).

  The Phase 2 query is a drop-in replacement — one new LEFT JOIN. No schema
  change to match_scores; no fork.

Brand sees ranked creator cards with match confidence bands.

CREATOR-FACING SURFACE (Voice Syndicate brief opportunities)
-------------------------------------------------------------

  SELECT brief_id, final_score
  FROM match_scores
  WHERE creator_id = $1
    AND final_score > $threshold
  ORDER BY final_score DESC
  LIMIT 5;

Default threshold: 0.55 for brand-side discovery; 0.65 for creator
notifications (asymmetry is intentional — brands see more candidates;
creators receive fewer, higher-quality opportunities).

Note: because the fidelity floor (0.45 default) is enforced at score
computation time, match_scores will never contain rows with cosine_sim below
the floor. The notification threshold of 0.65 is applied against final_score,
which is always ≥ cosine_sim × 1.00. The effective minimum cosine_sim for a
creator notification is therefore ≥ 0.65 / 1.25 ≈ 0.52, well above the floor.

The associated sports moment for each brief (FK on the brief record) is
surfaced as the creative prompt in the Voice Syndicate feed.

SECTION 5: BRAND BRIEF SURFACING TO CREATORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TRIGGER CONDITIONS
------------------

Three paths notify creators of relevant briefs, in priority order:

  1. New brief published (highest confidence):
     After compute_match_scores(scope: 'brief') completes, a downstream job
     notify_creator_matches queries:
       match_scores
       WHERE brief_id = $1
         AND final_score > 0.65
         AND creator_id NOT IN (already_notified_for_this_brief)
     Enqueues one creator_brief_notification job per eligible creator.

  2. Creator embedding refreshed (milestone-triggered):
     After remix milestones (5th, 10th, 25th), score recomputation may promote
     a creator above the 0.65 threshold for existing active briefs. If they
     cross the threshold for the first time, notify_creator_matches fires.

  3. Nightly re-engagement digest (reduced threshold):
     For creators with no remix activity in 7 days, a nightly job sends a
     consolidated digest of their top-3 matching active briefs at a reduced
     threshold of 0.50. This re-engagement surface operates at lower confidence
     but keeps inactive creators aware of marketplace demand.

RATE LIMITS
-----------

Enforced at the notification layer, not the score layer:
  - Max 2 brief notifications per creator per 7-day rolling window
    (across all three trigger paths)
  - Aspirational creators: max 1 per 14-day window
  - A brand brief that receives <20% acceptance rate across 50+ notifications
    is automatically flagged for human review and paused from further creator
    surfacing

CREATOR-FACING UX FLOW
-----------------------

Creators see a "Voice Opportunity" card in their home feed, visually distinct
from organic moment prompts.

Card shows:
  - Brand category (not brand name — revealed only after creator taps to
    expand and opts in)
  - The sports moment to react to
  - Estimated audience reach tier (bucketed, not absolute numbers)
  - Match confidence indicator: Good Fit / Strong Fit / Top Match
    (derived from final_score in three bands; raw score never shown)

Creator actions:
  - Accept: enters guided remix flow with brief context
  - Pass: permanent exclusion for this brief (written to brief_creator_responses;
    filters all future notification queries for this creator × brief pair)
  - Snooze (30 days): re-enters the notification queue after the snooze window

TRUST GUARDRAILS
----------------

  - Feed ordering rule: organic Voice Syndicate prompts always appear before
    Voice Opportunity cards in the same rendering cycle. Brands cannot buy
    position above organic discovery.
  - Brands cannot contact creators directly. All communication routes through
    the Opportunity Card system, which enforces rate limits and opt-out filters.
  - Opt-out by brand category: creators can suppress entire brand categories
    (e.g., gambling, alcohol) at the notification layer.

SECTION 6: EARNED RE-RANK LAYER — UPGRADE PATH [FIX 5]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE CORRECT ARCHITECTURAL VERDICT
-----------------------------------

The tournament judgment was framed as "embedding beats bandit." The correct
framing is: embedding now, learned re-rank later. The embedding approach is
not the permanent final state of the matching engine — it is Phase 1. Phase 2
is a learned re-ranking layer that attaches to the embedding shortlist once
sufficient conversion data exists to train it.

This is not a mitigation bolt-on. It is the designed evolution of the
architecture. Phase 1's embedding shortlist becomes Phase 2's candidate
generation layer. The re-ranker reorders within the shortlist; it does not
replace the shortlist. The engine never forks.

WHY THE EMBEDDING IS INSUFFICIENT LONG-TERM
--------------------------------------------

The formula is hand-tuned at design time. The 0.15 dark-social coefficient,
the 0.10 salience coefficient, the 0.45 fidelity floor — none of these update
from observed commercial outcomes. If a creator segment consistently surfaces
to brands and fails to convert, their match_scores do not degrade. The
match_scores table is refreshed on content signals alone, not campaign results.

As the marketplace matures and brand conversion data accumulates, the matching
engine will produce expensive misfires: high-scoring creators who consistently
fail to deliver campaigns, and lower-scoring creators who consistently convert
but are systematically under-surfaced. The gap widens silently.

The correct move is to define the upgrade path now, before the gap becomes
visible in revenue terms.

TRIGGER CONDITIONS FOR ADDING THE RE-RANK LAYER
-------------------------------------------------

All four conditions must be met before the learned re-ranker is promoted to
production:

  1. Data volume:
     ≥ 500 distinct (creator_id, brief_id) pairs with observed conversion
     outcomes recorded in brief_creator_responses. Conversion outcome = creator
     accepted the brief AND the campaign reached completion (remix delivered).
     Reason: below this volume, the re-ranker will overfit to the first few
     brands' preferences and degrade ranking quality.

  2. Brand diversity:
     ≥ 15 distinct brand campaigns completed with ≥ 10 creator conversions each.
     Reason: a model trained on one brand's preferences produces a brand-specific
     ranker, not a marketplace ranker. Diversity ensures the learned signal
     generalizes.

  3. Calibration drift threshold:
     Embedding-only ranking's precision@5 on a held-out validation set (20% of
     historical brief-creator pairs with known outcomes) falls below 60% in a
     quarterly evaluation. This is the signal that the static formula is
     diverging from what the marketplace actually values.
     Evaluation cadence: quarterly, automated, reported to the matching team.
     Trigger fires when drift is observed in two consecutive quarterly evals.

  4. Offline validation gate:
     The re-ranker candidate model must demonstrate precision@5 on the held-out
     validation set that exceeds the embedding-only baseline by ≥ 10 percentage
     points before promotion. This gate prevents a regression where an
     under-trained model degrades ranking quality.

ARCHITECTURE: HOW THE RE-RANK LAYER ATTACHES
---------------------------------------------

The re-ranker is a lightweight gradient-boosted model (e.g., XGBoost or
LightGBM) trained on features derived from the embedding shortlist + observed
outcomes. It does not replace the embedding engine.

  Input to the re-ranker:
    - cosine_sim (from match_scores.cosine_sim)
    - dark_social_ratio, normalized_salience (from creator_embeddings)
    - creator_conversion_rate: fraction of prior brief acceptances that reached
      campaign completion (from brief_creator_responses aggregation)
    - brand_category_affinity: creator's historical conversion rate broken out
      by brand category (from brief → brand FK)
    - recency_weight: time-decay on historical outcomes (more recent conversions
      weighted higher)

  Training signal:
    Label = 1 if creator accepted and completed the brief, 0 otherwise.
    Objective: pairwise ranking loss (LambdaRank or RankNet) to directly
    optimize ranking quality, not classification accuracy.

  Shortlist size: embedding generates top-50 candidates (Step 1–2); MMR
  selects the final top-N (default 20) from those 50 (Step 3); the re-ranker
  reorders the N=20 MMR-selected candidates. The re-ranker's input is the
  post-MMR set, not the pre-MMR pool. If N is raised for a brief tier (e.g.,
  N=30), the re-ranker's input size scales accordingly.

  Inference path:
    1. compute_match_scores job runs as before, writing cosine_sim and
       final_score to match_scores (unchanged).
    2. A new job compute_rerank_scores reads the top-50 final_score entries
       for a brief, assembles the re-ranker feature vector, runs inference,
       and writes rerank_score to rerank_scores (the Phase 2 table defined in
       Section 1 schema).
    3. The brand-facing API query uses the Phase 2 SELECT with COALESCE
       (already defined in Section 4). No other code changes.

  Retraining cadence: monthly, triggered by the nightly stale-score sweep job.
  Model version is stored in rerank_scores.model_version for auditability.

  Rollback: if the re-ranker's online A/B experiment shows no lift or degraded
  conversion after 30 days, the COALESCE in the API query falls back to
  final_score by expiring or dropping rows from rerank_scores. Zero schema
  changes required.

WHAT THE BANDIT WON AND WHERE IT GOES
---------------------------------------

The bandit (Approach 4) won tournament criterion 3 (dark-social diffusion
signal) because its per-cluster arm matrices could learn that Nigerian Pidgin
creators convert specifically for Telecom Africa briefs — a genuine advantage
that a static 0.15 dark-social coefficient cannot match.

This advantage is absorbed by the re-rank layer's brand_category_affinity
feature. Once sufficient data exists, the re-ranker learns the same cross-
cluster conversion patterns the bandit would have discovered online, but
offline (lower variance) and with the embedding's full language fidelity
guarantee as the shortlist foundation.

The bandit's reward loop is not "bolted on" — it is what becomes
creator_conversion_rate and brand_category_affinity in the re-ranker's
feature set. The embedding shortlist ensures no high-fidelity creator is
invisible to that learning process.

================================================================================
TOURNAMENT JUDGMENT: WHY EMBEDDING BEAT THE BANDIT
================================================================================

Embedding won because it commits to the product's core differentiator —
80+ language cultural fidelity — as a structural property rather than a
learned approximation. The multilingual sentence transformer places language
and cultural voice in continuous geometric space trained on hundreds of
millions of multilingual examples, far richer than the 32-dim matrix the
bandit could learn from MadLipz's still-thin remix graph. The cold-start
story is deterministic: a survey maps to label embeddings on a fixed blending
schedule using the same transformer, and worst-case is a conservative score,
not undefined behavior. Stack fit is clean: pgvector is a first-class Supabase
extension, pg-boss handles refresh jobs, Drizzle handles the schema, and
match_scores is a flat relation. Voice Syndicate unification is not an
architectural metaphor — it is literally the same table queried in two
directions, with nothing to synchronize and no drift surface.

The bandit won only on dark-social signal leverage (criterion 3) because its
per-cluster arm matrices could learn conversion patterns the embedding approach
cannot match with a static coefficient. This advantage is real but time-deferred:
it requires a trained model, which requires training data, which requires a
functioning marketplace first. The Section 6 upgrade path captures this
advantage once the data exists without forking the engine.

The correct verdict: embedding is the right foundation for a marketplace that
must be trustworthy from day zero. The learned re-rank layer is the right
evolution once the marketplace has enough signal to calibrate it.

APPENDIX: ELIMINATED APPROACHES AND DISQUALIFICATION REASONS
================================================================================

APPROACH 2: Collaborative Filtering — ELIMINATED
-------------------------------------------------
Constraint 2 FAIL: Language codes were added as "pseudo-labels in the item
space." The score formula (collaborative_score × profile_weight + 0.15 ×
salience_bonus) contains no language term. Language fidelity is not guaranteed;
it depends on whether the factorization organically clusters language codes.

Constraint 3 FAIL: Because language is a pseudo-label, replacing all language
codes with genre codes would produce an identical algorithm. The multilingual
creator base is not architecturally exploited.

Additional incoherence: Cold-start rule stated "profile_weight=0.4 means
aspirational creators don't appear in brand shortlists" — this is not entailed
by the weight value; the design provided no actual gating mechanism.

APPROACH 3: Semantic Graph / Spreading Activation — ELIMINATED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Fatal internal coherence failure (scoring mechanism mathematically broken):

The DARK_DIFFUSED edge type carries a decay coefficient of 1.10, described as
a "boost, not decay." Spreading activation computes activation as the product
of edge weights along a path (A(n) = A(source) × ∏ edge_weight × decay_coeff).
Any path containing multiple DARK_DIFFUSED edges produces unbounded activation
(1.10^10 ≈ 2.59 for 10 hops). The design specifies no activation ceiling or
hop-count limit for this edge type. Ranking is therefore unstable and
non-convergent as described.

Secondary coherence failure: BriefNodes are described as "ephemeral, not
persisted." The creator-facing Voice Syndicate path requires traversal to
"return BriefNodes that are active." Ephemeral nodes cannot be returned after
the traversal job completes — they are not in the graph. Direct contradiction.

APPROACH 5: Dark-Social Diffusion Graph + ML Re-Ranker — ELIMINATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Fatal internal coherence failure (primary novel claim invalidated):

The design's unique contribution is chain depth reconstruction via
parent_event_id: tracking how many hops a clip traveled through private
channels. But parent_event_id linkage requires knowing which click event
generated each subsequent share. Dark-social channels (WhatsApp, iMessage,
Telegram) do not transmit referrer headers — they structurally erase the
linkage data that parent_event_id chains depend on. The primary novel claim
of the design cannot be implemented using the mechanism described.

Constraint 2 FAIL: language_fidelity_bonus is defined as
  1.0 + 0.3 × (fraction of dark-social diffusion in brief's target language)
Maximum contribution: a 30% multiplicative bonus. Language is a capped
modifier on top of cluster-based scoring, not a first-class ranked dimension.
Additionally, dark_social_ratio_gate and salience_floor_gate are explicit hard
filters in the score formula — the approach uses gates despite the constraint
explicitly prohibiting treatment of language as a hard filter.
